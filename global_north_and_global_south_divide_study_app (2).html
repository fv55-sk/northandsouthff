1. Mandatory Slide Vocabulary ("Words To Go By")

Slides 4–10
FIRST WORLD High Tech & Wealth

What it means: Fully industrialized, technologically advanced, and wealthy economies.

Examples: USA, Japan, Germany, UK, Australia.
Exam Memory Trick: Think "Engineers & Factory Owners"—controls 80% of world income and owns 90% of global manufacturing.
THIRD WORLD Raw Material & Agriculture

What it means: Developing or semi-industrialized nations with lower living standards and high resource dependence.

Examples: Philippines, Ghana, Bolivia, Vietnam.
Exam Memory Trick: Cold War non-aligned states now referring to resource-supplying developing nations.
NEOCOLONIALISM Indirect Rule

What it means: Former colonies holding political freedom, but dominated economically and culturally by foreign powers.

Example: Foreign corporations controlling mining rights and national debt.
Exam Memory Trick: "No foreign armies present, but foreign banks control the budget." (Sukarno: "Colonialism in modern dress").
AUSTERITY Social Budget Cuts

What it means: Cutting state budgets for public health, education, and subsidies to pay off deficit or foreign debts.

Example: IMF mandating healthcare cuts in exchange for bailout loans.
Exam Memory Trick: "Tightening the national belt by starving public social services."
ABSOLUTE POVERTY Survival Threat

What it means: Severe lack of basic survival necessities: food, clean water, shelter, sanitation, and healthcare.

Key Note: Threatens biological survival, unlike relative poverty (income relative to peers).
Exam Memory Trick: Core target of UN Millennium Development Goal #1.
REGIONALISM Neighbor Integration

What it means: Geographic neighbors uniting economically and politically to boost bargaining strength.

Examples: ASEAN in Southeast Asia, European Union (EU).
Exam Memory Trick: "Neighboring states grouping together so global superpowers can't dictate to them."

2. Major Theories of International Relations

Slides 14–20
Politics-Centric • Anarchy

REALISM

Power, Survival, and National Interest

  • International System: Inherently disorderly and anarchic (no global authority over states).
  • Primary Actors: States only. They act out of pure self-interest and national survival.
  • Power Logic: Zero-sum game ("Relative gains"). A state either has power or has none; no middle ground.
  • View on Inequality: Global inequality is a natural result of military and economic power imbalances between sovereign states.
Exam Key Phrase:
"States are rational actors locked in a perpetual struggle for power in an anarchic world."
Politics-Centric • Cooperation

LIBERALISM

Interdependence, Institutions, & Free Trade

  • Core Assumption: Rejects pure power politics. Believes cooperation and peace are possible through mutual benefits ("Absolute gains").
  • Key Actors: States, UN/IMF supranational authorities, TNCs, and NGOs.
  • Mechanism: Global trade routes, international law, and diplomatic institutions.
  • View on Inequality: Free market access and capital flows will eventually bridge the North-South gap.
Exam Key Phrase:
"Economic interdependence and rule-based institutions replace conflict with mutual cooperation."
Economics-Centric • Class Struggle

MARXISM

Capitalist Exploitation & Core-Periphery

  • Core Assumption: Global instability stems directly from capitalist class struggle and economic exploitation.
  • 4 Causes of Instability: Capitalism, resource exploitation by the wealthy few, unequal wealth distribution, and institutionalized injustice.
  • Structure: Powerful Core (Global North) extracts wealth, cheap labor, and resources from the Periphery (Global South).
Exam Key Phrase:
"Inequality is not an accident—it is built into global capitalism to benefit wealthy core states."
Social Construct • Ideas & Norms

CONSTRUCTIVISM

Identities, Social Meanings, & Practice

  • Core Assumption: International politics is shaped by shared identities, values, and social norms rather than just raw military strength.
  • Famous Motto: "Anarchy is what states make of it." (Alexander Wendt).
  • View on Inequality: Global North/South categories are social constructs reinforced by historical discourse and historical perceptions.
Exam Key Phrase:
"State interests and international structures are created and altered through social interactions, norms, and identities."

3. Pro-Globalist & Western Hegemony Paradigms

Slide 21
GLOBALISM Manfred Steger

Core Idea: Global economic integration is not just inevitable due to technology—it is a normative international goal that all nations should pursue.

🧠 2-Word Memory Anchor: Moral Goal
Exam Shortcut: Globalization is presented as a "must-have good thing" for human progress.
MODERNIZATION THEORY W.W. Rostow

Core Idea: Development happens in 5 linear stages of economic growth based on a society's domestic capacity to produce and consume goods.

🧠 2-Word Memory Anchor: Ladder Steps
Exam Shortcut: Assumes poor nations are just "at an earlier step" on the same Western path.
END OF HISTORY Francis Fukuyama

Core Idea: Western liberal democracy and free-market capitalism are the ultimate endpoint (telos) of human political evolution.

🧠 2-Word Memory Anchor: Final Winner
Exam Shortcut: Capitalism won the ideological war; all nations will eventually adopt it.
THE LEXUS & OLIVE TREE Thomas Friedman

Core Idea: A binary choice: embrace high-tech global free trade (the Lexus) or hold onto traditional isolation (the Olive Tree) and suffer stagnation.

🧠 2-Word Memory Anchor: Adapt or Die
Exam Shortcut: Either plug into global markets or get left behind in poverty.
CLASH OF CIVILIZATIONS Samuel Huntington

Core Idea: Post-Cold War conflict will NOT be driven by money or politics, but by cultural and religious identity fault lines between civilizations.

🧠 2-Word Memory Anchor: Cultural Wars
Exam Shortcut: Conflicts occur along civilizational borders (e.g., Western vs. Islamic vs. Sinic).
